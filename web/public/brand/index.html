orc brand book To the board

orc

A task orchestrator for Claude Code. The short name reads as “orc”, and we do not argue with that: we build the name on it. A control room with a chief at the desk, and a squad of agents that goes to work on its own and comes back with the result on its own.

The eyes breathe: at least one agent is working right now.

Name

The name is written in lowercase: orc. Never “Orc” or “ORC” in the interface: it is the name of the command as typed in the terminal, and on screen it is set in monospace. The full expansion is needed once, at first mention for a new person: “orc, a task orchestrator for Claude Code”. After that, just orc.

We say “orc” out loud and are not shy about it. In interface copy the word “orc” never appears on its own: the dispatcher, agents, tasks and branches live there. The orc is how the control room looks and behaves, not what its parts are called.

The idea

A squad that goes to work on its own.

The orc of fantasy games is not a villain but a worker: blunt, direct, tireless and always part of a squad. That is an exact description of what orc does: agents take tasks from the queue, each goes off to its own worktree and comes back with a result, and they rarely need a human: to answer a question, untangle a conflict, restart something that fell over.

Three visible decisions follow from this idea:

  • The mark is an orc's head, not an abstract glyph. It is recognizable at 16 pixels and needs no backing plate.
  • The orc's eyes glow while the dispatcher is alive. This is the only place where the brand meets the system state, and it obeys the general interface rule: color means state.
  • A few orc glyphs — horn, campfire, banner, axe, tusk — appear where they carry meaning and do not replace the familiar status icons.

The mark

A skull with a heavy brow and ears, a slit mouth, and two tusks growing from the lower jaw and overlapping the slit. Everything is built on a 32×32 grid of straight segments: the mark stays sharp at any scale and in any color. The silhouette is drawn in one color (currentColor), the eyes are a separate layer.

Five outlines: skull, ears, jaw, tusks, eyes. The tusks and the jaw share the skull's color, so in the silhouette the tusks read only where they overlap the mouth. This is deliberate: the fewest details, the most recognizability.

The clear space is a quarter of the mark's side on every side. Nothing goes inside it except the name in the lockup.

Sizes

The minimum is 16 pixels: at that size both tusks are still visible. Below that the mark is not used at all.

Variants

VariantWhen
Silhouette + glowing eyesInterface, favicon, lockup. The main one.

What not to do

The eyes mean state

The interface has one palette rule: the chrome is colorless, any colored spot is a task state. The single exception is the phosphor --live, the dispatcher's heartbeat. The orc's eyes glow in that same color under that same rule, so they do not break the system: they repeat its signal where it is visible even when the panel is collapsed.

EyesMeaningWhere
DimmedThe dispatcher is paused or not responding, or there is no workSidebar, empty states
GlowingThe dispatcher is alive, agents are freeSidebar, “About orc”
Glowing and breathingAt least one agent is working right nowSidebar, in the same rhythm as the header dot

The eyes never have another color. Red eyes for a failure are out: a failure is shown by the card, not by the mark.

Palette

The brand has no color of its own on top of the interface palette. Graphite is the surface, ink is the mark, phosphor is life. Everything else colored on screen belongs to task statuses and is not governed by the brand.

In the light theme ink and canvas swap places, phosphor darkens to #16a34a (contrast of at least 4.5:1 on white), and bone to #a89a78. Status shades are described in docs/design/ui-design.md; they are not part of the brand, and the brand does not touch them.

Typography

IBM Plex Sans for everything a human reads. IBM Plex Mono for machine strings: the name orc, task numbers, branches, paths. Monospace here means “you can copy this into a terminal”, and the product name follows the same rule: it is the command.

orc
Wordmark: IBM Plex Mono 600, tracking −0.02em, lowercase only
Waiting for me 3
Headings and captions: IBM Plex Sans 600, regular case, no all caps

The “mark + name” lockup: the gap between them is half the mark's side, the name is aligned to the optical middle, and the font size is three quarters of the mark's side. The “agent control room” tagline is used only in the sidebar and in the lockup for documents; in window headers it is superfluous.

Glyphs

Five icons in the lucide language (24 grid, 2 stroke, round caps) that lucide does not have. Each one stands where it carries meaning. Task status icons stay lucide: they are seen most often, and they have to be familiar.

Voice

The orc speaks briefly, with verbs, and to the point. Not pathos and not a roar: the confidence of someone who knows what to do. Humor is allowed in exactly two places: empty states and the brand book. Never in errors, confirmations or instructions to agents: there the text explains what happened and what to do.

DoDon't
Agents are freeThe horde rests by the campfire 🔥
The task has no description. Write itThe orc did not understand what is wanted of it
No connection to the server: check that it is runningThe chief does not answer the horn's call
No projects yet. Add a repository — agents will work in its branchesEmpty! Add a project right now!!!

Interface words are not renamed to fit the metaphor: the dispatcher stays the dispatcher, agents stay agents, a task stays a task. The orc lives in the mark, the glyphs and the character of the text, not in the vocabulary.
